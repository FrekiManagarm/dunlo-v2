## First Maker Comment

Hey Product Hunt,

I am Mathieu, founder of Dunlo.

I built Dunlo because failed payments in SaaS often get treated like a retry problem, but a lot of them are really communication problems. A customer with an expired card, a blocked bank charge, or an authentication step does not always need more pressure. They need the right explanation and the right action.

Dunlo connects to Stripe and turns decline reasons into a recovery workflow:

- failure-aware emails for expired cards, insufficient funds, SCA, and bank declines;
- smarter retry timing around the reason the payment failed;
- founder review for high-value or sensitive accounts;
- recovered revenue tracking so teams can see what was saved.

The beta is free while I shape the product with early SaaS teams.

I would love feedback from founders who use Stripe subscriptions:

- Where does payment recovery break in your current workflow?
- Which failed-payment cases still require manual founder follow-up?
- What would make this trustworthy enough to run in your SaaS?

Thanks for taking a look.

## Launch-Day Reply Bank

### "How is this different from Stripe Smart Retries?"

Stripe Smart Retries helps decide when to retry a payment. Dunlo focuses on the customer-facing recovery layer around that retry: why the payment failed, what the customer should do, which email to send, when to pause for founder review, and how much revenue was recovered.

### "Do you store card numbers?"

No. Dunlo does not store card numbers or CVC data. Card updates should happen through Stripe-hosted flows.

### "Who is this for?"

Stripe-first SaaS founders and operators who already see failed subscriptions, involuntary churn, or manual failed-payment follow-up in their workflow.

### "Is the beta free?"

Yes. Dunlo is free during beta while I work closely with early teams.

### "What happens after beta?"

The paid plan is not final yet. The current priority is to learn from real Stripe teams, recover revenue reliably, and keep the workflow trustworthy for customers.
